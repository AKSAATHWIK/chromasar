# Round 1 feedback log - fill this in AT the 2:30 panel

Round 2 at 4:00 is the scored round, and what it scores is **how well you engaged with
Round 1**. Not how much you agreed - the brief says so explicitly:

> "It is not necessary for you to take feedback at face value. You may defend yourself as
> to why you haven't taken the feedback comments 100%."

So the winning move is not compliance, it is a **visible, reasoned response to every
point**. Walking into Round 2 able to say "you raised four things, here is what we did
with each, and here is the one we deliberately did not do and why" is worth more than
silently implementing three of them.

## How to run this

**One person writes, the others talk.** Decide who before 2:30. The writer captures
verbatim where possible - paraphrasing loses the thing the judge actually cared about.
Ask them to repeat anything you did not catch; a panel would rather repeat itself than
be misquoted at 4:00.

**Before they leave, read the list back to them.** Ten seconds, and it turns a vague
conversation into an agreed list. It also signals you are taking it seriously, which is
the thing being assessed.

**Then triage immediately.** You have ~90 minutes. Sort into:
- **DO** - cheap and clearly right. Do these first.
- **PARTIAL** - right in spirit, too big to finish. Do the visible half, say what remains.
- **DEFEND** - you disagree, and have a measured reason. These are worth points if the
  reason is real. "We measured it and it does not work" is a defence. "We ran out of
  time" is not - that goes under PARTIAL.

At 4:00, lead with this table. Do not make them ask.

## The Round 2 opening line

Rehearse this once at ~3:50, out loud, with the table in front of you:

> "You gave us N points at 2:30. We acted on X of them - here they are. On one we
> disagreed, and here is the measurement behind that."

Then walk the table top to bottom. Show, do not describe: if you changed the app, have it
already running on the change.

## What NOT to do

- Do not rewrite the whole approach in 90 minutes because one judge disliked something.
  A half-broken pivot at 4:00 scores worse than a working system with a reasoned
  disagreement.
- Do not silently drop a point you disagree with. An unaddressed item reads as "did not
  listen." A DEFEND row with a reason reads as "thought about it."
- Do not promise anything for the finale you cannot show a plan for. See the 36-hour plan
  in TEAM_NOTES.md - point at it rather than improvising a timeline.

Situation report

    Why this works
    Water is a specular reflector — it bounces the radar pulse away from the satellite, so open water returns almost no energy and appears near-black. The exception is floodwater under vegetation, which double-bounces off trunks and returns bright: the case a pure threshold misses and a learned model catches. Probabilities are temperature-calibrated, and permanent water from the JRC layer is subtracted so the number reported is new water, not rivers that were always there.

    Honest limitations
    Radar does not measure colour. Part of this output is inference from texture and polarisation; part is the model guessing from learned priors. Running the generator repeatedly with dropout live gives many plausible answers — agreement means evidence, divergence means invention. This model is the weaker half of the project: it reproduces water and high-contrast structure, and tends toward a muddy conditional mean elsewhere. The confidence map is what makes that honest rather than hidden.

    Method

    SAR satellites fire radar pulses and measure the echo, so they work at night and through cloud — the only sensor that keeps working through the Indian monsoon. The output is a grayscale map of surface texture, not a photograph, and reading it takes years of training.

    Numbers above are the Sen1Floods11 official test split at 512 px, ResNet34 encoder with ImageNet weights. The threshold baseline uses dual polarisation (VV < −18.5 dB OR VH < −24 dB), tuned on validation only. Train/validation splits are by scene, not patch — adjacent tiles of one place on both sides would inflate the score.

    Probabilities are temperature-calibrated (T = 1.368), reducing expected calibration error from 0.029 to 0.016 with IoU unchanged.
